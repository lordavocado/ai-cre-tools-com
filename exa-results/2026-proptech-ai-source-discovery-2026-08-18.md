# 2026 PropTech and real-estate AI source discovery

Research date: 2026-08-18

Research window: 2026-01-01 through 2026-08-18

Scope: global sources that can reveal new AI software for commercial real estate, multifamily, property operations, development, construction, and the wider built environment

## Executive conclusion

The strongest new discovery channels are not general startup databases. They are operator-backed programs where a product must survive contact with owners, managers, brokers, contractors, or public land and planning bodies.

The best additions to the existing monitoring set are:

1. **NAR REACH Commercial** for revenue-stage CRE and multifamily software.
2. **Aroundtown ATechX** for products tested against major European real-estate portfolios.
3. **Geovation's PropTech track** for UK land, property, planning, and location intelligence.
4. **RET Ventures' AI Accelerator** for multifamily leasing and operations.
5. **Suffolk BOOST** and **NXT Activate** for emerging AEC software; both have important cohort announcements still ahead in 2026.
6. **PwC Scale PropTech** for more mature scaleups, with an important date-quality caveat.
7. **Official vendor newsrooms plus specialist CRE/AEC media** for product launches that accelerators miss.

This pass produced a strong screening queue led by EQUIRE, Provision, Leamur, Muvan AI, Emulate Cities, LeasingAI, Grotto AI, Duxre, Propstreet, and brightplace. A subsequent publication-grade review admitted nine products and held back three; the complete outcome appears at the end of this report.

## Direct-launch sources accelerators do not cover well

Accelerator monitoring alone misses launches from established teams and self-funded vertical-AI companies. The following source stack produced several of the strongest findings:

- **Official product newsrooms and release pages**: the best evidence for availability, release date, capabilities, and integration claims. Examples include [Provision's Scope Agent release](https://provision.com/announcements/provision-launches-scope-agent) and [Green Street's CRE MCP server](https://www.greenstreet.com/mcp-server-commercial-real-estate/).
- **Specialist CRE media**: Commercial Observer, Institutional Real Estate Inc., CRE Daily, Bisnow, The Real Deal, and Propmodo. These are valuable for finding a launch, but every claim should be checked against the vendor's official product surface. [Commercial Observer's Duxre coverage](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/08/duxre-ai-brief/) is a good example.
- **AEC and construction media**: Construction Dive, ENR, BuiltWorlds, AEC Magazine, and contractor or BIM ecosystem newsrooms. These are especially useful for integrations and general-availability releases.
- **Press wires**: PR Newswire, Business Wire, and GlobeNewswire provide excellent date discovery but are vendor-authored evidence. They should never be the only product-quality source.
- **Investor portfolio and thesis pages**: RET Ventures, MetaProp, Navitas, Pi Labs, Foundamental, and Zacua are high-signal when the investor explains the product, customer type, and adoption rationale rather than merely listing a logo.

## Sources with lower immediate yield

- **General accelerator classes** can be worth one review when backed by a large property company, but Techstars Tokyo's 2026 class showed that sponsor identity does not guarantee PropTech output.
- **General MIPIM awards** predominantly recognize projects, buildings, and transactions. Regional pavilions and curated startup sessions are more useful for software discovery.
- **Net-zero programs** surface important built-environment innovation, but many cohorts are dominated by equipment, advanced materials, energy infrastructure, and hardware outside this directory's software focus.
- **Residential agent competitions** frequently produce CRMs, lead-generation products, mortgage tools, and homebuyer apps. They should be screened separately from the CRE-first queue.
- **Program copy is not first-party product evidence**. Several cohorts describe a company as AI-driven even when the company's own site does not.
